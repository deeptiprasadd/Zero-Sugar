The four checks, and how each came out

Each check and what would happen if it failed were written down before it ran. All four failed, and the tool uses the fallback fixed for each.

1. Does the video's content beat follower count alone?no

No. For accounts the models had not seen, no model predicted better than chance, and content added nothing to reach. So the tool shows what a typical video gets, not a forecast.

2. Can the Instagram score be read as a chance?no

No. Its level shifts each time the model is refitted, so it is shown only as a position among the 120 Instagram videos, labelled exploratory.

The study found no difference between the platforms that it could rely on (about 3 times higher on Instagram, but anywhere from 0.5 to 17 times). The two platforms' videos were also chosen differently - most Instagram videos were picked by hand, and 58 of the 72 YouTube videos come from one global channel - so the platforms cannot be separated from how their videos were chosen.

What it cannot tell you

Posting time, paid promotion, how the platform's own systems spread a video, and anything about sales or reach beyond likes and comments are not measured. Instagram does not publish reliable view counts, so no Instagram reach is shown.

The example videos

The form's examples are six videos from the study's own collection that no model was fitted on: five Zero Sugar videos left out of the 192 only because their likes were hidden or their comments switched off, and one regular Coca-Cola Reel that shows how the tool warns about a video unlike the ones it learned from. Each was measured once in advance with its real details.
