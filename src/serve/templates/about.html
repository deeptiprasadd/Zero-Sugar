The four checks, and how each came out

{% set g3 = card.outputs.base_rate.g3_interval_coverage %}
1. Does the video's content beat follower count alone? No. For accounts the models had not seen, no model predicted better than chance, and content added nothing to reach. So the tool shows what a typical video gets, not a forecast.
2. Can the Instagram score be read as a chance? No. Its level shifts each time the model is refitted, so it is shown only as a position among the 120 Instagram videos, labelled exploratory.
3. Do the ranges hold as often as they claim?Not at first ({{ '%.0f'|format(100 * g3.overall) }}% instead of 80%), so they were widened by {{ '%.2f'|format(g3.widen) }} times. Instagram's then hold {{ '%.0f'|format(100 * g3.after_widening.instagram) }}% of the time; YouTube's still only {{ '%.0f'|format(100 * g3.after_widening.youtube) }}%, so they are too narrow for YouTube.
4. Does the tool measure exactly as the study did? Pending: the final check on 10 of the 192 videos is still to run. On two test videos outside the 192, all 73 measures matched exactly.

The study found no difference between the platforms that it could rely on (about 3 times higher on Instagram, but anywhere from 0.5 to 17 times). The two platforms' videos were also chosen differently - most Instagram videos were picked by hand, and 58 of the 72 YouTube videos come from one global channel - so the platforms cannot be separated from how their videos were chosen.

What it cannot tell you

Posting time, paid promotion, how the platform's own systems spread a video, and anything about sales or reach beyond likes and comments are not measured. Instagram does not publish reliable view counts, so no Instagram reach is shown.
